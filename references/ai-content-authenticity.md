# AI Content Authenticity Reference

Use this reference when auditing copy, marketing content, documentation, UI text, or other human-facing content in an AI-assisted project.

The supplied video presents these as signs that content may have been generated or heavily assisted by AI. Treat them as review signals, not proof of authorship. Do not claim that text is AI-generated from style alone.

## Observed style signals

Audit for:

- repetitive or formulaic phrasing
- stock opening phrases such as "delve into" or "dive deep" when they add no meaning
- rhetorical questions used as generic engagement devices
- cliche framing such as "here's the truth" when it is not needed
- unnaturally perfect grammar that conflicts with the intended voice
- excessive emoji use in prose or marketing copy
- contraction patterns that feel mechanically uniform rather than natural to the brand voice
- over-explaining obvious points
- generic content that contains no project-specific insight
- lack of distinctive examples, opinions, observations, or evidence
- content that is technically polished but says little that is specific to the product or audience

## Content quality rules

- Do not treat any single style signal as proof of AI authorship.
- Prefer specific, useful, product-grounded writing over generic filler.
- Preserve the intended voice when the project has an established brand voice.
- Remove repetitive framing and unnecessary explanations.
- Add real examples, product details, evidence, constraints, or user-facing value where they are genuinely known.
- Never invent personal experience, customer stories, results, research, credentials, or opinions to make copy feel human.
- Proofread deliberately, but do not force artificial typos or grammatical mistakes merely to appear human.
- Avoid adding emojis simply to make copy appear casual.
- Keep terminology consistent across pages, UI states, emails, and documentation.

## Verification

When reviewing content:

1. Identify generic or repetitive passages.
2. Check whether claims are supported by the product or supplied evidence.
3. Check whether examples are real and relevant.
4. Compare important copy with the project's actual features and user flows.
5. Rewrite only where the change improves clarity, specificity, usefulness, or brand consistency.

This is a content-quality and authenticity check, not an AI detector.
